# Essential Logic OHIF Viewer - User Manual

## 1. Introduction
The Essential Logic OHIF Viewer is a sophisticated, web-based radiology viewer entirely wrapped in a modern, GPU-accelerated "Glassmorphism" interface. It is designed for clinical practitioners to review DICOM imagery seamlessly across desktop and mobile devices.

## 2. Navigation & Layout
### Study List Page
Upon logging in, you are greeted with the **Study List Page**.
- **Patients/Studies:** Displays clinical studies currently assigned to your account or stored in the connected Orthanc PACS. Click the `>` arrow on any Study Card to open it.
- **Upload Feature (Mobile & Desktop):** You can upload local DICOM files by clicking the primary floating `+` button in mobile or the Upload button in the top right on Desktop.
- **Repositories Tab:** Access public medical imaging databases (like TCIA, OpenNeuro, fastMRI) for research.
- **AWS Public Tab:** Directly streams studies from public AWS cloud storage.

When clicking a study, a spinner labeled "Loading study data..." will appear until the DICOM volume stream is ready.

## 3. The Viewer Interface
Once inside a study, you are presented with the **Glass Layout**.

### 3.1 The Top Glass Toolbar
The toolbar running across the top of the screen provides master controls:
- **Layout Switcher:** Allows splitting the screen to view multiple sequences (e.g., 1x1, 1x2, 2x2 grids).
- **Mode Switcher:** 
  - *Viewer:* Standard radiological review.
  - *Segmentation:* Dedicated view for AI masks and brushing.
  - *Annotation:* Dedicated view for reporting and bounding boxes.
- **Cine Loop:** Triggers the Cine Player (movie mode) for scrolling through thick stacks of images automatically without scrolling your mouse.

### 3.2 The Left Sidebar (Tools)
The primary action panel docks on the left:
- **Window/Level (W/L):** Left-click and drag in the viewport to adjust contrast and brightness.
- **Zoom & Pan:** Select these to magnify or move the image.
- **Scroll Bar:** Scroll through slices in the active sequence.

### 3.3 The Right Sidebar (Data & Panels)
The right panel dynamically changes depending on your selected Mode (from the Top Toolbar):
- **Image Info (Data):** Displays critical DICOM header metadata (Patient Demographics, Modality, Slice Thickness, Equipment).
- **Study Browser:** Switch between different series or prior studies of the current patient visually.
- **Measurements/Annotations List:** Displays a tabular list of any lengths or ROIs you have drawn on the scan.

## 4. Measurement & Annotation Tools
To use measurements, select an annotation mode (or select the specific tool from the left sidebar if running in advanced mode):
- **Length Tool:** Click the starting point of the abnormality and drag to the endpoint. It instantly calculates millimeters (mm) based on pixel spacing.
- **Bidirectional Tool:** Used for lesions; draw the long axis followed by the short axis.
- **Arrow / Text:** Drop a pointer and type a note onto the pixel data. 
All annotations are automatically persisted to the cloud, allowing you to load "Pending Annotations" across sessions.

## 5. Working with AI Segmentation
If a 3D mask exists for the study (e.g., tumor bounds generated by MONAI):
- Select the **Segmentation** mode in the Glass Toolbar.
- The AI mask will overlay on top of the original pixel data, tinted in a specific color (like Red for Liver, Green for Lesion).
- You can toggle the opacity or visibility of these layers in the right-side Segmentation UI.

## 6. AI Radiology Copilot
Sitting in the bottom-right corner of the viewer is your **AI Copilot**.
- **How to use:** Click the Bot icon. The chat drawer will slide out. 
- **Context Awareness:** The bot is already aware of the study you are viewing (e.g., it knows you are looking at a 55-year-old male's Chest CT).
- **Examples of Questions:**
  - *"How do I draw a bidirectional measurement?"*
  - *"Summarize the DICOM metadata for me."*
  - *"What are the standard guidelines for managing a 4mm lung nodule in a non-smoker?"*

## Note on Mobile Usage
The viewer is 100% functional on tablets and smartphones. Gestures (pinch-to-zoom, two-finger pan, horizontal swiping for tools) replicate desktop mouse behaviors. Toolbars horizontally scroll seamlessly to prevent clipping on small screens.
